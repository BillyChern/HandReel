HandReel: Fast Hand-Object Reconstruction from Video without Per-Frame Pose Sampling

Shi Chen1,2,Jeffrey Ichnowski1

1Robotics Institute, Carnegie Mellon University,2Intuition

HandReel turns a video of a hand moving an object into one 3D mesh of the object and its position and rotation over time.

On 9 Ego4D segments, DO AS I DO's reconstruction stage restarted for every segment and took 130× as long per segment as HandReel did in batches. Abstract

Videos of people handling objects could teach robots manipulation, if we could cheaply recover each object's 3D shape and motion from monocular video. A rigid object keeps its shape, so one mesh can serve many frames. HandReel processes video in short windows and poses one mesh per window from point tracks instead of sampling a pose in every frame. It builds each mesh from one masked frame, aligns it to estimated depth and takes metric scale from the estimated right hand. On Ego4D, HandReel processed many windows in one run that loaded models once. When the DO AS I DO pipeline's reconstruction stage ran its released code and restarted for every window, it took far longer per window than a window's share of HandReel's run. On held-out HOI4D with ground-truth prompts, HandReel's object-centre error was lower than that stage's in the median window when a failure to deliver counts as an error. There, HandReel delivered every analysed window. In a first application, which turns one video into a robot trajectory, that pipeline would take longer with its own reconstruction than with HandReel's, by a sum of separately measured stage times. In a second application, which converts many videos with one retargeting policy, every video needs the reconstruction that HandReel shortens.

Method

HandReel works on one short video segment (up to about 10 s in the paper's experiments) at a time, in three steps, and it never asks a generative model for the object's position. First, it builds one 3D mesh of the object and reuses it for the whole segment. Second, it gets a first guess of where the object is over time by following points on the object through the video. Third, it adjusts all of those positions and the object's size together until they agree with the depth maps and the followed points. HandReel takes the real-world scale from the estimated right hand, so the right hand must be visible. In the paper's experiments, it returned a result only when its quality check passed.

Reconstruction time

In our experiments on Ego4D, DO AS I DO's reconstruction stage took about 30× as long as HandReel when both processed one segment at a time, and 130× as long per segment when it restarted for every segment while HandReel processed segments in batches.

Applications

DO AS I DO turns a video of a person handling an object into a robot motion, and reconstruction is its first stage. By stage times that we measured separately on Ego4D, its full pipeline would take about 3× as long per segment with its own reconstruction as with HandReel's.

When one retargeting policy serves many videos, every video still pays for reconstruction.
